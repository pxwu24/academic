    Definitions

    Echelon form
    1. Any rows of all zeros are at the bottom.
    2. Each leading entry (first nonzero entry of a row) is in a column to the right of the leading entry in the row above.
    3. Every entry below a leading entry is 0.
    Reduced echelon form

    Echelon form, and also:

    1. Every leading entry is 1.
    2. Each leading 1 is the only nonzero entry in its column.
    Pivots and solutions
    • A pivot position is where a leading entry sits in an echelon form of the matrix (a leading 1 in its reduced echelon form). A pivot column contains a pivot position, and a pivot is the nonzero number there.
    • Two matrices are row equivalent if row operations turn one into the other.
    • For an augmented matrix, variables in pivot columns are basic; the others are free.
    • A system is consistent exactly when the last column is not a pivot column. A consistent system has a unique solution exactly when there are no free variables.
    Row operations
    • Replacement: add a multiple of one row to another row.
    • Interchange: swap two rows.
    • Scaling: multiply every entry of a row by a nonzero number.
    Finding A−1A inverse

    Row reduce [A | I]. If A is row equivalent to I, then [A | I] is row equivalent to [I | A−1A inverse]. Otherwise A is not invertible.
